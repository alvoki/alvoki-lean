# Verification status

The stock sequential Linux engine uses a segmented WAL, v2 checkpoint manifests and v3 run files. Lean proves a legal client history for executions admitted by the mixed trace. Several source-level theorems derive such executions from the actual public call and reached callbacks. Covering every allowed production execution is the open end-to-end obligation. ### Durable batches

A batch uses one append. The WAL and staged marker are synced before the marker rename, followed by the directory barrier. An interruption before marker publication recovers the previous selection under the append and namespace assumptions. After a late publication error a joint crash sample may instead retain the whole submitted batch. The reported mixed trace also covers clean reopen, repeated failed opens and process exit. ## Optimized reader and search

The machine-index lower bound refines natural-number search for monotone keys. The optimized eight-byte load agrees with the stock reader for an in-bounds extent and a machine-sized array. Ordered v1 records within format and machine bounds round-trip through encoding, parsing and decoding. The baseline, scalar and indexed encoders agree on the bytes. Materialized and streaming checksums agree on the decoded rows. Verus covers the sorted-key search core. Independent fixtures cover its integration into the packed Rust reader.
